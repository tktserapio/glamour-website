GLAMOUR Group

Mission

Our research sits at the intersection of artificial intelligence, economics, and game theory.

We aim to build AI agents that make effective decisions in today’s world, where they interact strategically with other intelligent agents, both AI and human.

Strategic settings

Markets and negotiation

We study online advertising auctions, bargaining, and supply-chain negotiations where each decision depends on what other agents may do next.

Learning dynamics

Multi-agent adaptation

We ask when simultaneous learning converges, when it destabilizes, and how interacting systems can still produce efficient social outcomes.

Embodied examples

Coordination in the wild

Example domains include vehicles negotiating traffic, distributed robotic control, and language-model agents operating in shared strategic environments.

Our Research

Research Areas

As AI agents increasingly make decisions on behalf of people (e.g., scheduling meetings, bidding in auctions, coordinating robots, or negotiating contracts), the key challenge is ensuring that the interactions of multiple autonomous systems are stable, efficient, and socially desirable.

Multiagent Learning: Learning stable behavior in interactive systems

In multiagent systems, each agent's best strategy depends on what the other agents are doing. We study whether simultaneous learning can converge to stable game-theoretic behavior and when it can produce cooperative, socially desirable outcomes.

  • Convergence to equilibria in repeated interaction
  • Cooperation and incentive alignment across agents

Algorithmic Game Theory: Designing markets, auctions, and strategic mechanisms

We analyze the structure of strategic interaction and ask when equilibria can be computed efficiently. This connects directly to the design of online auctions, marketplaces, and autonomous bidding, trading, and negotiating agents.

  • Efficient equilibrium computation
  • Long-run behavior of strategic learning agents

Strategic LLMs: Building harnesses for language models in strategic settings

Language models are still unreliable at strategic decision-making, even in settings that look simple on paper. We build agent harnesses and evaluation environments that help LLM-based agents reason, coordinate, and act effectively in multiagent worlds.

  • Strategic reasoning with language-model agents
  • Evaluation environments for coordination and negotiation
