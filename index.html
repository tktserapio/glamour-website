Research Areas

As AI systems increasingly make decisions on behalf of people (e.g. bidding in auctions, coordinating robots, or negotiating contracts) the key challenge is no longer just making one model accurate. It is ensuring that many autonomous systems interact in ways that are stable, efficient, and socially desirable.

Multi-Agent AI

Most AI algorithms assume a fixed environment. We study environments where everyone is learning at the same time. Examples include:

  • autonomous vehicles negotiating traffic,
  • online advertising auctions,
  • supply-chain negotiations,
  • distributed robots.

The challenge is that one agent's optimal strategy depends on what every other agent is doing, making the environment continually changing ("non-stationary").

Multi-Agent RL

Questions include:

  • Can agents learn to cooperate? When do they compete?
  • Will learning converge to stable behavior?
  • Can learning algorithms reach game-theoretic equilibria?

Algorithmic Game Theory

We study ideas like:

  • Nash equilibria,
  • correlated equilibria,
  • no-regret learning

LLMs and Game Theory

We aim to use LLMs for strategic decision-making in multi-agent environments.

  • What harnesses or context can be used to guide LLM agents in multi-agent settings?
  • How can we use LLMs to model strategic environments?
