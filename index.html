Research Areas

Our research sits at the intersection of artificial intelligence, economics, and game theory.

The E-GLAMOUR (Economics - Games, Learning, Agents, Modeling, Optimization, Uncertainty Research) Group aims to build AI agents that make effective decisions in today’s world, where they interact strategically with other intelligent agents, both AI and human. As AI agents increasingly make decisions on behalf of people (e.g., scheduling meetings, bidding in auctions, coordinating robots, or negotiating contracts), the key challenge is ensuring that the interactions of multiple autonomous systems are stable, efficient, and socially desirable.

Multiagent Learning

In multiagent systems, each agent's best strategy depends on what the other agents are doing. When multiple agents are learning at the same time, some key questions include:

  • Will learning converge to stable behavior, such as game-theoretic equilibria?
  • Can agents learn to cooperate and arrive at socially-desirable outcomes?

Applications:

  • Building game-playing agents
  • Computing equilibria in economies

Algorithmic Game Theory

Can AI and algorithmic thinking be used to improve social outcomes in multiagent systems, while taking account of individual agent’s incentives? Key questions include:

  • Can equilibria be computed efficiently?
  • Can we explain the long-run behavior of multiple strategic learning agents?

Applications:

  • Designing online auctions and marketplaces
  • Building autonomous bidding and trading agents

Strategic LLMs

We aim to use LLMs for strategic decision making in multiagent environments.

LLMs have been reported to be notoriously bad at strategic decision making. For example, they cannot play the game of Tic-Tac-Toe well, even though its solution is a part of their training data. We are endeavoring to build agent harnesses that steer LLM agents towards effective decisions in multiagent settings.

Applications:

  • Automated negotiation
