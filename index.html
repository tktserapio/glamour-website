Designing agents for multiagent environments and understanding the dynamics they create.

Research Areas

Our research sits at the intersection of artificial intelligence, economics, and game theory.

We aim to build AI agents that make effective decisions in today’s world, where they interact strategically with other intelligent agents, both AI and human. As AI agents increasingly make decisions on behalf of people (e.g., scheduling meetings, bidding in auctions, coordinating robots, or negotiating contracts), the key challenge is ensuring that the interactions of multiple autonomous systems are stable, efficient, and socially desirable.

Multiagent Learning

In multiagent systems, the challenge is that each agent's best strategy depends on what the other agents are doing. When multiple agents are learning at the same time, some key questions include:

  • Will learning converge to stable behavior, such as game-theoretic equilibria?
  • Can agents learn to cooperate and arrive at socially-desirable outcomes?

Algorithmic Game Theory

We analyze how AI and algorithmic thinking can be used to improve social outcomes in multiagent systems, while taking account of individual agent’s incentives. Concretely, this looks like designing online auctions, marketplaces, and autonomous bidding and negotiating agents. Key questions include:

  • Can equilibria be computed efficiently?
  • How can we explain the long-run behavior of multiple strategic learning agents?

Strategic LLMs

We aim to use LLMs for strategic decision making in multiagent environments. LLMs have been reported to be notoriously bad at strategic decision making. For example, they cannot play the game of Tic-Tac-Toe, even though its solution is a part of their training data. We are endeavoring to build agent harnesses that guide LLM agents in multiagent settings.
