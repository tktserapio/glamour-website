GLAMOUR Group

Our research sits at the intersection of artificial intelligence, economics, and game theory. We aim to build AI agents that make effective decisions in today’s world, where they interact strategically with other intelligent agents, both AI and human.

Examples include:

  • vehicles negotiating traffic
  • online advertising auctions
  • supply-chain negotiations
  • distributed robotic control

Research Areas

As AI agents increasingly make decisions on behalf of people (e.g., scheduling meetings, bidding in auctions, coordinating robots, or negotiating contracts), the key challenge is ensuring that the interactions of multiple autonomous systems are stable, efficient, and socially desirable.

Multiagent Learning

In multiagent systems, the challenge is that each agent's best strategy depends on what the other agents are doing. When multiple agents are learning at the same time, some key questions include:

  • Will learning converge to stable behavior? E.g., game-theoretic equilibria?
  • Can agents learn to cooperate and arrive at socially-desirable outcomes?

Algorithmic Game Theory

Key questions include:

  • Can equilibria be computed efficiently?
  • How can we explain the long-run behavior of multiple strategic learning agents?

Applications include designing online auctions and marketplaces on the one hand, and autonomous bidding, trading, and negotiating agents on the other.

Strategic LLMs

LLMs have been reported to be notoriously bad at strategic decision making. For example, they cannot play the game of Tic-Tac-Toe, even though its solution is a part of their training data. We are endeavoring to build agent harnesses that guide LLM agents in multiagent settings.
