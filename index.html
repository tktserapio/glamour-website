Our Research

Our research sits at the intersection of artificial intelligence, economics, and game theory.

We aim to build AI agents that make effective decisions in today’s world, where they interact strategically with other intelligent agents, both AI and human. As AI agents increasingly make decisions on behalf of people (e.g., scheduling meetings, bidding in auctions, coordinating robots, or negotiating contracts), the key challenge is ensuring that the interactions of multiple autonomous systems are stable, efficient, and socially desirable.

Multiagent Learning

Read more

In multiagent systems, each agent's best strategy depends on what the other agents are doing. We study whether simultaneous learning can converge to stable game-theoretic behavior and when it can produce cooperative, socially desirable outcomes.

  • Convergence to equilibria in repeated interaction
  • Cooperation and incentive alignment across agents

Algorithmic Game Theory

Read more

We analyze the structure of strategic interaction and ask when equilibria can be computed efficiently. This connects directly to the design of online auctions, marketplaces, and autonomous bidding, trading, and negotiating agents.

  • Efficient equilibrium computation
  • Long-run behavior of strategic learning agents

Strategic LLMs

Read more

Language models are still unreliable at strategic decision-making, even in settings that look simple on paper. We build agent harnesses and evaluation environments that help LLM-based agents reason, coordinate, and act effectively in multiagent worlds.

  • Strategic reasoning with language-model agents
  • Evaluation environments for coordination and negotiation
